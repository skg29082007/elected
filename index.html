The Election Lifecycle

A clear, chronological breakdown of how democracy functions.

Months Before

🏛️ Candidate Filing & Primaries

Candidates officially file their intent to run for office. Political parties hold primary elections to select their nominees. Voters within each party choose who represents them in the general election.

Ongoing Deadlines

📋 Voter Registration

Citizens must register before the deadline to appear on the official voter roll. Requirements include proof of citizenship, minimum age of 18, and residency proof. Some regions allow same-day or automatic voter registration.

Weeks Before

📣 Campaigning & Debates

Candidates travel, advertise, and hold public rallies to earn votes. Official debates allow candidates to present and defend their positions. Campaign finance laws strictly regulate how money is raised and spent.

Election Day

🗳️ Voting

Registered voters visit their designated polling place to cast their ballot. Voting methods include in-person, mail-in, and early voting options. Official observers from all parties monitor the process for transparency.

Days After

🔢 Counting & Canvassing

All ballots including provisional and mail-in are officially counted. The canvass is a formal review of every vote cast in the jurisdiction. Recounts may be triggered automatically when margins fall below 0.5%.

Weeks After

✅ Certification & Transition

Government authorities formally certify the final election results. All legal challenges must be resolved before certification is complete. The winner is inaugurated and power transfers peacefully — democracy's hallmark.
